What this is. A feed on invasion of the non-human world, in both directions it happens. The invaded are always non-human — wild animals, plants, fungi and the systems they make. Native in this feed never means people; it means the species that were already there. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

Human incursion. Roads cut into forest, concessions granted over habitat, mining and drilling at the frontier, trawling and seabed licensing, tourism and expeditions into places that were remote, unsurveyed or left alone. A road on its own is not this feed's business; a road into a forest is, which is why both halves have to be present before a story is kept.

Species invasion. Organisms arriving where they did not evolve, carried in ballast water, hull fouling, the pet and horticulture trades, aquaculture escapes and timber; the pathogens that travel with them — chytrid, white-nose, high-path avian influenza in wild birds, African swine fever in boar; the ranges shifting as waters warm; the wildlife and plants that give way; and the eradication and biosecurity work aimed at all of it.

Which is which. Every row is labelled, and the Invasion filter separates them. A story can be both — a new road that also carries a weed front up it — and then it appears under both.

Scale. Being on subject is not enough. A single species turning up in one county reads as a register entry, and it is dropped. A story has to reach past one place: a global or regional spread (2 and 1), a systemic mechanism — collapse, cascade, irreversible, unprecedented, established across (2), a number (1), a consequence for people or a system (1), or a generalising study (1). Below two points nothing enters, and the status line reports how many were turned away as too local on the last harvest.

Evidence. Each story that gets in is then scored on what it carries: a documented incursion — a first detection, a concession granted, an outbreak, construction begun (2); institutional material — IUCN, IPBES, CBD, a national inventory, satellite monitoring, peer review (2); a measured extent in hectares, kilometres or per cent (1); a forward projection (1); a named place (1); and a primary source (1). The pips on each row show the score out of five and the words beside them name what earned it. At three or more a story counts as well documented, and the Evidence filter narrows the list to those — it hides nothing permanently, it just sets aside the passing mentions.

What is refused. Invasion in its military and criminal senses — armies, home invasions, pitch invasions, the film — and alien in its immigration and cinema senses. Eradication in its public-health sense, so that polio and malaria campaigns stay out. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. An incursion story written without any habitat vocabulary will be missed. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused or dropped as too local. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
